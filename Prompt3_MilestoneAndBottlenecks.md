In a Game Development environment, bottlenecking can very easily lead to drastic slowdowns in development time. When you are assigning tasks, there's a possibility that two developers might end up with overlapping tasks, or one might require the other as a prerequisite for the developer to even start their project. This is bottlenecking, as one developer is working on something that a second developer requires for their own project, the second developer is forced to wait or dedicate their time to helping the first developer, this is usually extremely inefficient.

To combat bottlenecking, two things have become standard in the industry; Rapid Prototyping and Parallel Development. The intention behind both of these is to restructure the way we assign tasks, to split the project into phases which the whole team will work in together, and to make sure developers are always working on tasks that are removed from each other so nobody gets in the way of somebody else's tasks.

You can think of these two processes in construction terms. Rapid Prototyping is the act of splitting the work up into different layers, like completing the foundation of a building before moving onto the first floor, then the second, and so on. Likewise, Parallel Development is equivalent to erecting columns and building supports to hold up future floors on this building. A typical plan would end up like this; During the Alpha stage different teams are developing concept art, basic movement systems, a testing stage, and a concrete file structure, all working parallel to each other. During the Beta stage, different teams are developing official art assets to be implemented, more complex and specific systems such as environment interaction or gunplay, etc, all in parallel. The number of these stages and the exact tasks will vary drastically depending on the game concept and the needs of the team.
